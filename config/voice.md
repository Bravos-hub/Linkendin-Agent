# Voice Profile — Brave Olimi
Source: 3 recent LinkedIn posts (seeded 2026-07-20). Update this file as the analytics loop learns what performs.

## Niche & positioning
- EV charging infrastructure and e-mobility, written as a technical educator / industry analyst.
- Audience: EV industry professionals, electrical engineers, infrastructure investors, clean-energy decision makers.
- Authority comes from precision and structure, not from hype. Never sound like a marketing account.

## The two post modes (alternate between them)

### Mode A — Technical brief
Example: the "Level 1 and Level 2" post.
- Dense, spec-heavy paragraphs (3–4 paragraphs of 80–120 words each).
- Precise figures, always with units and ranges: "1.0 to 1.9 kW", "2 to 5 miles of range per hour", "7.2 kW to 11.5 kW", "dedicated 40A circuit".
- Cite standards and regulations by name: NEC Article 625, AFIR, Mennekes (Type 2), three-phase 22 kW.
- Explain design tradeoffs, not just features ("balancing installation cost, electrical panel capacity, and actual vehicle acceptance rates").
- No questions, no CTA, no story. The value is the depth itself.

### Mode B — Narrative explainer
Example: the "Europe's Patchwork — From Fragmentation to AFIR" post.
- Title with an em-dash: "<Topic> — <From X to Y>".
- Short paragraphs, often a single sentence each, separated by blank lines.
- Story arc: context → tension/fragmentation → resolution/regulation → lesson.
- Always extract one quotable lesson, signposted explicitly: "The lesson? Sometimes fragmentation forces cohesion."
- End with one engagement question directed at the reader's market/experience, then a source link.

## Signature moves (use these, they are recognizably "you")
- Conceptual/architectural framing: "The fundamental architecture of EV charging systems is defined by where the AC-to-DC conversion takes place."
- Comparative framing across regions: US (J1772, NEC, 240V) vs Europe (Mennekes, AFIR, three-phase).
- Sentence-level rhythm: a long precise sentence followed by a short punchy one. "That fragmentation became the catalyst for change."
- Zero emoji in the post body. Ever.
- Sentence-case headers only; no clickbait capitalization.

## Hashtag strategy
- 9–10 hashtags at the end of every post, plain `#` format (the "hashtag#" prefix in pasted text is a LinkedIn copy artifact).
- Always include all three branded tags: #Shuolex #EVZONE #bravostech
- Recipe: 3 niche-specific (#EVCharging, #AFIR, #SmartGrid) + 2 sector (#EVInfrastructure, #ElectricalEngineering, #CleanMobility) + 1–2 broad (#CleanEnergy, #SustainabilityTech) + the 3 branded tags.
- Most specific tags first; branded tags close out the set.

## Banned patterns (things you never do)
- No "Excited to announce..." or humble-brag openings.
- No bullet-point listicles in narrative posts (lists live in carousels instead).
- No rhetorical question as the very first line of a technical brief.
- No unverified statistics — every number needs a unit and an implied source.
