A $30,000 EV pickup with a native NACS port.

That sentence would have sounded like marketing fiction two years ago.

Ford's upcoming midsize truck is reportedly being built that way from the start.

Technically, native NACS means the vehicle-side controller and charging inlet are designed around Tesla's connector form factor and signaling protocol, not adapted down from CCS1. The result is a single-path charging architecture that can plug into V3 and V4 Supercharger cabinets without a mechanical adapter, with the handshake, current negotiation, and thermal management handled by the vehicle's own battery management system. It removes the adapter losses and compatibility uncertainty that still complicate public charging for many CCS1 vehicles.

The lesson? The charging experience is becoming a product spec, not an afterthought.
