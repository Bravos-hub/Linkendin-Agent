Mitsubishi's 2027 Eclipse Sportback EV is essentially a rebodied Nissan Leaf, and its charging architecture mirrors that lineage. The 75 kWh battery pack charges from 10 to 80 percent state-of-charge in roughly 35 minutes on a suitable DC fast charger, while the same pack delivers a maximum range of about 303 miles in the Leaf.

What makes the hardware interesting is the dual-port layout. The passenger-side fender carries a NACS inlet rated for up to 150 kW at Tesla Supercharger stations, or CCS-equivalent speeds with an NACS-to-CCS adapter. The driver's side carries a J1772 inlet for Level 1 or Level 2 AC charging at homes, workplaces, or legacy public stations.

The real design question is not the port count, but the cost-to-convenience balance. A budget-positioned EV at an expected starting price near $31,500 does not need premium 350 kW charging, but it does need broad network access. By adding both NACS and J1772, Mitsubishi trades a small increase in packaging complexity for compatibility with the two largest North American charging ecosystems.
