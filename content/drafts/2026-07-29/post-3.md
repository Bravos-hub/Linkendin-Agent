Utilities have managed air conditioners for decades.

Managing public EV charging is harder because the driver has no relationship with the utility.

Connecticut PURA just showed how to scale past that problem.

The agency evaluated four Cycle 1 pilots under the Innovative Energy Solutions Program and selected AmpUp's Adapt & Earn managed charging project for statewide deployment. The 18-month pilot with Eversource Energy enrolled more than 1,000 charging ports across 200 locations and eight commercial customer segments. During a regional heat wave, the platform shifted charging to off-peak periods and delivered cost savings of about two-thirds compared to the cost of the peak electricity supply that was displaced.

AmpUp's approach is technically lightweight: it does not require vehicle telematics, advance enrollment, or additional hardware. Participants receive wallet credits in the AmpUp app in exchange for brief charging pauses during peak demand periods.

The lesson? Grid flexibility works when the driver barely notices it.
