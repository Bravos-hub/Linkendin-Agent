Engineering velocity is not typing speed.

At GM's autonomous-driving division, engineers spend only 15% of their time writing code.

The other 85% is analysis, triage, testing, and verification.

AI agents now handle much of that overhead.

The result is roughly three times as many merged pull requests, faster releases, and fewer escaped defects.

GM divided the work into three loops — simulation, road testing, and post-deployment monitoring — and asked one question in each: where is the longest bottleneck? Agents were connected to internal tools and petabytes of vehicle data via customized MCP servers and version-controlled 'skills' that define how to perform specific tasks. Permissions mirror the engineer, so accountability stays human while execution scales. Background agents run machine-learning experiments in parallel, with tests reviewed before release.

The lesson? Agents amplify engineering most when they are woven into the workflow, not dropped on top of it.
