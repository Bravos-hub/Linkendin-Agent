DC-coupled energy hubs — from grid constraint to fleet enabler

Fleet electrification runs into a wall when the local grid cannot support dozens of vehicles charging at once.

Battery storage and on-site solar help, but every AC-DC conversion step throws away energy.

DC-coupled smart energy hubs change the equation by keeping generation, storage, and vehicle charging on the same DC bus.

Cenex modelling of the 3ti Papilio3 V2X DC FastHub shows peak site demand dropping by 5 kW to 150 kW, depending on the site and operational profile.

Annual energy cost savings at office, leisure, and depot sites on two-rate tariffs sit between £2,600 and £3,700.

High-demand factory sites can save up to £18,000 per year.

The architecture also cuts conversion losses, improving overall efficiency and renewable utilization.

The lesson? When grid capacity is the bottleneck, the answer is not always a bigger connection — sometimes it is a smarter topology.
