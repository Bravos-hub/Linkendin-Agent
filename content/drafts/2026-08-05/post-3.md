Same price. New plug.

The 2027 Chevy Blazer EV holds last year's pricing — $44,700 for the base LT — while adding a native NACS port.

That single hardware change opens Tesla's Supercharger network without an adapter, part of a GM transition that began with the 2026 Cadillac Optiq and now gives its drivers access to more than 250,000 chargers across North America.

The engineering significance sits at the charge-port interface. A native inlet removes the adapter from the connection chain — one less failure point in the mating cycle and the communication handshake — while GM continues to supply adapters so drivers retain access to CCS Level 2 charging at home and on legacy public networks. With an 85 kWh pack and up to 312 miles of EPA-estimated range in front-wheel-drive trims, the vehicle's road-trip utility now depends less on any single network's build-out pace.

The lesson? In an infrastructure transition, the connector is the strategy.
