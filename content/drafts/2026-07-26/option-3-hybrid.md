# Option 3 — Hybrid

Some trucks haul freight across continents.

This one moves it fifty metres at a time.

And it just went electric.

Kalmar Ottawa's T2 EV terminal tractor is now working for WTC Group in Canada, supported by Transport Canada's iMHZEV incentive program. The drivetrain is sized for the job it actually does: a continuous-duty electric motor with up to 454 kW of peak output, a modular lithium-ion battery in 105, 140, or 210 kWh capacities, and a gross combination weight rating of 195,000 lbs. Because the truck never leaves the yard, charging is a depot scheduling problem — not an infrastructure waiting game.

That is the pattern worth watching in fleet electrification. The vehicles that electrify first are rarely the glamorous ones. They are the ones whose energy demand per shift is predictable, whose routes end where they began, and whose operators control the plug.

The lesson? Electrification follows the duty cycle, not the headline.
