The Two Car Clubs — From Export Races to Home Turf

For years, the global EV race was measured in exports.

China just changed the scoreboard.

Beijing's carbon-peaking action plan requires NEVs to reach 30% of the entire national fleet by 2030 — not 30% of sales, 30% of every car on the road. That implies well over 100 million NEVs, more than double today's level.

Hainan has gone further: the first Chinese province to finalize a 2030 ban on new fossil-fuel car sales, targeting 45% NEV fleet share.

Thailand is reading from the same script. BYD dominates its EV market, and incentives now reward exports — turning the country into an EV base for ASEAN.

Plot the major car-exporting nations by BEV share of home registrations and two clubs emerge: China and Thailand above 30–40% and climbing; Japan and the US below 10% and flat.

The lesson? Industrial strategy is built at home first — exports are the dividend, not the engine.
