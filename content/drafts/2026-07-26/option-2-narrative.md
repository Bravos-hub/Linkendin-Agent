The Terminal Tractor — From 1943 Diesel to Battery-Electric

In 1943, Kalmar Ottawa invented the terminal tractor — the squat, single-purpose truck that shuffles trailers and containers around ports and yards.

For decades, that machine ran on diesel.

It idled most of its life, burned fuel at its least efficient operating point, and became so dependable that fleets stopped asking whether it could be anything else.

Now WTC Group, a Canadian logistics firm, is putting battery-electric T2 EVs to work, backed by Transport Canada's iMHZEV incentive program.

The truck delivers up to 454 kW of peak power, draws from a modular battery of up to 210 kWh, and can pull a gross combination weight of 195,000 lbs.

No public charging network required — the yard is the network.

The lesson? Electrification follows the duty cycle, not the headline.
