London has already seized more illegal e-bikes this year than in all of 2025.

Over 2,500 in six months, against 2,241 in the whole of last year.

The difference is not more officers. It is better measurement.

The Met now uses portable roadside dynamometers — rolling roads — to test whether an e-bike exceeds the UK's legal limit of 15.5 mph (25 km/h) of motor assistance, making it one of only two UK forces with the equipment. Oversized battery packs and throttles that propel without pedaling are the tell-tale signs that trigger an inspection. One recent operation near Tottenham Court Road pulled 29 e-bikes and seven e-scooters in a single day, with more operations planned through the summer.

That is the part of micromobility regulation most cities skip. A speed limit you cannot measure at the roadside is a suggestion. The dyno is what turns it into a standard.

The lesson? Regulation without measurement is just paperwork.

Should roadside dyno testing be standard equipment in every city with e-bike rules?
