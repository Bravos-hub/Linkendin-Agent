Kenya is slow on electric cars.

Its electric buses tell a different story.

While private adoption lags, the bus segment has found its mechanism: pay-as-you-drive. Operators acquire buses at minimal upfront cost and pay per kilometre, with charging and real-time monitoring bundled into the contract.

Under the hood, this is an engineering decision as much as a financial one. A financed fleet charges on predictable duty cycles, so depots are designed around scheduled low-to-mid-power DC charging with OCPP-managed load balancing rather than opportunistic high-power sessions. The analytics layer that monitors every bus doubles as the billing layer. Energy delivery, kilometres driven, and revenue collected become one data stream — and that contracted revenue is exactly what makes the next charging hub financeable.

The lesson? Financing architecture and charging architecture are the same problem, and Kenya's bus operators are solving them together.
