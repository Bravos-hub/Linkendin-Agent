Kenya's EV Story — From Missing Drivers to Financed Electrons

Kenya is, by most measures, one of the slower markets in Africa for electric car adoption.

And yet its electric bus segment is one of the most interesting on the continent.

The tension is easy to explain. Buyers face high upfront costs and thin public charging. Fleet operators face the same math, multiplied by every vehicle in the yard.

The resolution came from financing, not hardware. Pay-as-you-drive turned the electric bus into an energy-as-a-service product: minimal upfront cost, then a per-kilometre fee bundling the vehicle, the charging, and real-time monitoring of every bus.

That shift changed who can participate. A bus owner no longer needs to finance a battery and a charger. They need a route and a contract.

Now the partnerships are compounding; each depot makes the next one easier to finance.

The lesson? In emerging markets, infrastructure follows the business model — not the other way around.
