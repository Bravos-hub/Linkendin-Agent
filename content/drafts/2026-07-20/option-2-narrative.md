Europe's Charging Buildout — From Chicken-and-Egg to Infrastructure First

For years, the EV debate ran in circles: drivers would not buy without chargers, and operators would not build without drivers.

Europe broke the loop by regulating it away.

AFIR set two hard targets — public charging capacity scaled to each national BEV fleet, and 150 kW fast chargers every 60 km along the main road networks.

The results are now in.

By March 2026, every EU country except Malta met its fleet-based target.

The bloc closed 2025 with 1.1 million public chargers — five times the 2020 figure.

And 20 of 27 countries have already hit their 2027 target for regional road coverage, eighteen months early.

The network was built ahead of demand, and demand followed.

The lesson? When infrastructure leads, adoption stops being an act of faith.

Is coverage still the bottleneck in your market — or has the constraint moved to price transparency and reliability?
