# Option 3 — Hybrid

1.1 million public chargers. Fivefold growth since 2020. One country left behind.

Europe's charging network did not follow the EV market. It preceded it.

Under AFIR, each member state must hold at least 1.3 kW of public charging capacity per BEV in its national fleet, and by the end of March 2026 every country except Malta complied — with the aggregated national targets overshooting the bloc requirement by 180%. The distance-based mandate tells the same story: 79% of the TEN-T Core network now meets the target of a 150 kW fast charger every 60 km, and T&E estimates that just 70 strategically placed stations would push compliance to 90%.

The lesson? Targets written into law did what market signals alone would not.
